Test Case for our Saccade Detection feature 

Saccades are rapid, ballistic eye movements that occur when the visual 
system shifts focus from one point of interest to another. 
	 
In this project, saccades are extracted from processed eye-tracking data 
using a velocity-based detection approach grounded in standard 
eye-movement research. 
	 
Gaze direction is represented as a sequence of normalized 3D vectors, 
and saccadic motion is identified by computing the angular change between 
consecutive gaze samples. 
	 
This angular displacement is converted into angular velocity (degrees per 
second) using the known sampling rate of the eye-tracking system. 
	 
Samples exceeding a configurable velocity threshold are classified as 
saccadic, while lower-velocity samples are treated as fixations. 
	 
Consecutive saccadic samples are grouped into individual saccade events, 
from which metrics such as duration, peak velocity, and movement 
amplitude are derived. 
	 
Threshold values are intentionally parameterized to allow tuning based on 
experimental context, with an initial default of approximately 40 degrees 
per second, which is commonly used in VR eye-tracking studies to balance 
sensitivity and noise rejection.
